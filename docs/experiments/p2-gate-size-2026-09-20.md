# P2 — the gate's size under a true null · #115 · 2026-09-20

**The gate promotes a candidate identical to the champion 28% of the time.** Under
`strict` that falls to 1%, and no caller passes `strict`.

Read against the terms fixed in
[`p2-seed-sd-population-2026-09-20.md`](p2-seed-sd-population-2026-09-20.md) §5b,
written before this ran. **Its falsification condition 3 fires** — see §4. Nothing
was trained and nothing promoted.

Reproduction: `python pipeline/scripts/power_analysis.py --size`.

## 1. What was run

`evaluate_promotion` is a deterministic function of two summary dicts, so the null
is simulated over the decision function rather than over retrains. Both arms are the
same procedure: every gated metric is a mean over that arm's members, drawn at the
per-member spread measured on the re-baseline's TESS slice —

| metric | mean | per-member seed sd |
|---|---:|---:|
| ROC-AUC | 0.9074 | 0.00372 |
| Brier | 0.1248 | 0.00426 |
| ECE | 0.0582 | 0.01148 |
| recall @1% FPR | 0.2311 | 0.03028 |

The recall figure reproduces that run's own `pooled_gate_recall_seed_sd` to five
decimals, which is how we know this protocol matches the trainer's. The real
`cv_summary.json` is the schema, so nothing is rejected on shape.

10,000 draws per cell; the Monte Carlo se on a rate near 0.28 is **0.45 pp**, and
cells share a seed so differences between them are paired and tighter than that.

**`blocked_contrast` is stubbed.** It runs `PERMUTATION_DRAWS = 5000` permutations
on every call and its result is only appended to `reasons` (`promotion.py:759-761`),
so it cannot change a verdict — but it is **99.6% of the runtime**, 77 ms of the
77.8 ms per call. Stubbing it took the study from 104 minutes to 6 seconds. Recorded
because stubbing the function under test needs saying out loud.

**REJECT near 50% is deliberate and is not the finding.** `promotion.py:808` returns
REJECT for any `cand_auc <= champ_auc`, with the documented reason that a tie is not
a reason to replace a deployed model. Half of all null draws are ties or losses, so
half of all REJECTs are by design. The 6–16 pp above 50% is the Brier, ECE and
recall tolerances firing.

The anomalous number is PROMOTE.

## 3. Why the fall-through happens

The floor is `2 x se` and `unresolved_against` marks UNRESOLVED only inside
`[floor/1.5, floor x 1.5]` = `[1.33 se, 3 se]`. A **positive** margin below
`floor/1.5` is outside the band, no criterion has rejected it, and
`promotion.py:917` returns PROMOTE. So the PROMOTE region under a null is
`(0, 1.33 se)` plus the genuine tail beyond `3 se`.

That region is a function of the floor's **width**, not of whether the floor is
correct. Widening the floor widens the fall-through.

## 4. Falsification — the seed sd pre-registration's condition 3 fires

Its condition 3: *"The gate's measured size moves further from nominal after the
correction than before. If matching the error term to the estimand does not improve
the gate's size, the argument in §4 is wrong and the change is not justified by
it."*

At 5 v 5 — the allocation that correction exists for — non-strict false-PROMOTE
moves **23.4% → 29.8%**, against a nominal of about 2.3% for a 2-se rule. That is
6.4 pp further from nominal, roughly 14 Monte Carlo se, and UNRESOLVED gives up
almost exactly what PROMOTE gains (20.9% → 14.5%). At 5 v 1 it moves 28.3% → 28.5%,
inside Monte Carlo noise, consistent with §2a's finding that the borrowed champion
term dominates and the candidate's population is nearly inert there.

**Reported as falsified, not re-specified, per rule 6.** The correction is **not
justified by the size argument**. Whether it remains justified by the
estimand-matching argument alone — STAT 293's rule that the denominator's `E(MS)`
must match the numerator's — is a different question from the one that was
pre-registered here, and it needs its own terms rather than a quiet widening of
these.

The prediction recorded in §5b before this ran was *"of order 40%, not of order
2%"*. Measured 28.3%. **Directionally right, numerically high by about 12 pp**: the
Brier, ECE and recall tolerances absorb part of the fall-through, which the AUC-only
geometry does not account for.

## 5. What actually controls the size

`strict`. It takes false-PROMOTE from 28.3% to 1.1%, and UNRESOLVED absorbs the
difference. The mechanism is the paired-folds alarm — under a null the candidate
wins at most half its folds about half the time, and `strict` turns that alarm into
UNRESOLVED instead of advice.

**`strict` defaults to False and the only caller is `promotion_gate.py`, which
passes `args.strict` from a flag that defaults off** (`promotion_gate.py:110`). I
did not trace what the weekly refresh passes; that is worth checking and is not
established here.

This is a sizing mechanism that exists, works, and is off.

## 6. What this does not establish

- Nothing about power. A correctly sized gate that cannot detect a real improvement
  is no better; #115's other half is untouched, and P2.1's MDEs still stand.
- Nothing about whether `strict` should be the default. It moves the failure from
  false promotion to UNRESOLVED at 32–43%, and a gate that is unresolved
  two-fifths of the time under a null is not usable unattended either. Both are
  reported separately here for that reason.
- The null is simulated at one operating point — the re-baseline's TESS means. It
  is not swept over margins, so this measures size and not the full operating
  characteristic.
