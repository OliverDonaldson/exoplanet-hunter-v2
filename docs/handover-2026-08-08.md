# What this is building, and why

## The product

Exoplanet Hunter does **vetting**, not detection. Detection is finding a
periodic dip where no ephemeris is known; SPOC already runs that over all TESS
2-minute data, and that is where TOIs come from. Vetting is deciding whether a
*known* signal is a planet or a false positive — an eclipsing binary, a
background blend, a systematics artefact.

The deployment use is **ranking candidates for follow-up**. Telescope time is
the scarce resource; the product's job is to put the targets most likely to be
real planets at the top of an astronomer's list, and to show why.

End to end: NASA catalogue → validation gates → `tf.data` pipeline → calibrated
5-fold CNN ensemble → promotion gate → FastAPI scoring → React vetting console.
It serves live and interactively on a 2 GB Fly machine (peak 548 MB) at roughly
$1–4/month, which is a deliberate constraint — it rules out anything that needs
several GB of weights resident.

## What is already true

`ca906040` — a 9-dim dual-view CNN, 227,641 parameters, served since
2026-07-19 — is a working vetting model:

| slice | ROC-AUC | recall @1% FPR |
|---|---:|---:|
| TESS *(the deployment population)* | 0.9100 | 0.307 |
| Kepler | 0.9915 | 0.829 |
| K2 *(zero-shot)* | 0.9348 | 0.191 |
| all | 0.9523 | 0.439 |

**The roadmap is not an attempt to rescue a broken model.** It is an attempt to
fix five *measured* pathologies in a model that already works.

## The five defects the whole roadmap answers

Every stage traces back to one of these. None was guessed; each has a number.

1. **The score does not track transit evidence.** `corr(probability, transits
   actually caught) = −0.048`. Two targets with identical folded views can
   differ by a factor of three in how much real evidence they contain — DV's own
   counts show a median observed/expected ratio of 0.29, and only 12% of reports
   caught every transit their ephemeris predicts. Folding destroys that
   distinction.

2. **The model partly scores the star, not the transit.** With no injection at
   all, **26.4% of hosts still pass threshold** — 46.7% of planet hosts against
   12.3% of false-positive hosts. That is the model recognising the kind of star
   that tends to have planets, which is not vetting.

3. **Scalar summaries of diagnostics add nothing.** A 13-dimensional
   vetting-auxiliary retrain moved AUC by **−1.3e-5**. Compressing an
   odd/even test or a centroid shift to one number throws away what the test
   actually shows — so the diagnostic *views* have to be fed instead.

4. **The headroom is on the mission we serve.** TESS 0.906 against Kepler
   0.989. Kepler is nearly solved and is 0% of the deployment population.

5. **The labels themselves are biased, and no architecture can reach it.**
   Observation baseline correlates **+0.278 with the ground-truth label**, and
   **+0.387 on TESS** — *above every model*. TESS confirmed planets have a
   median baseline of 1,495 days against 430 for false positives. The mechanism
   is confirmation bias in the catalogue: a target observed across many sectors
   accumulates the follow-up that promotes it to confirmed, while a briefly
   observed one stays a candidate. **For the deployment use this actively
   defeats the purpose**, because it promotes targets that already got attention
   over under-observed ones that may deserve it.

## How the remaining stages answer them

| stage | answers | how |
|---|---|---|
| **6** recall variance | *the meta-defect* | the criterion that rejects every run has no error bar; give it one |
| **7** branch attribution | 1, 2, 3 | does per-transit and per-diagnostic evidence actually move the control-arm host-pass rate off 26.4% |
| **8** labels and negatives | **5** | the only stage that can touch the largest defect, because the signal is in the labels |
| **9** difference-image branch | 2 | a centroid shift under the transit is the direct test of "is this even the star we think it is" |
| **10** Optuna re-tune | 4 | extract what is left once the architecture and distribution are settled |
| **11** serving parity | *delivery* | make the per-branch reasoning visible through `/score`, so a ranking can be justified |

Defect 3 was already answered structurally in stage 2 — the eleven views exist
and the branches consume them. What stage 7 decides is whether they *earn their
place*, which is a different question from whether they were built.

# What "finished" means

**By the time stage 12 (UI) begins, Exoplanet Hunter should be a complete
product and the UI should be pure presentation.** Concretely, all of these must
be true:

1. **A promotion decision has been made on evidence.** Either a branch model
   passes the gate on TESS AUC *and* recall @1% FPR against the re-baselined
   incumbent, or the branch line is closed in writing and `ca906040` — or its
   stage-10 retune — stays served. Both are finished states. Only "we never
   found out" is not.
2. **Every number has an error bar.** AUC has one (`seed_sd 0.0081`,
   `fold_sd 0.0094`). After stage 6, recall @1% FPR has one too. No criterion
   decides anything on a margin smaller than its own noise.
3. **The control-arm host-pass rate has moved off 26.4%, or is explained.** This
   is stage 7's specified criterion and the sharpest single measure of whether
   the model vets the transit rather than the star.
4. **Ranking is not driven by observation baseline.** Stage 8's interventions
   have been measured against the +0.278 label correlation, and whatever
   residual remains is quantified and stated rather than unknown.
5. **The score is a probability.** Calibrated, with `score_std` persisted and
   surfaced per candidate — not a ranking number wearing a probability's
   clothes. (ExoMiner's own tooltip concedes theirs "is NOT a probability"; this
   is a real differentiator and it already works.)
6. **Every score can be explained.** `/score` returns per-branch contributions
   via occlusion, so a shortlist can be justified per target rather than
   asserted. This is what the UI will have to display, and it must exist before
   the UI is designed around it.
7. **The evaluation is reproducible from artefacts.** Already largely true —
   every run-1 number regenerated exactly during the audit — and it stays true.

Stage 12 then has one job: present per-candidate vetting evidence that already
exists, well. If the UI stage finds itself needing a number the API cannot
produce, a stage before it was not actually finished.

## The honest version of "success"

**The project's value does not depend on the branch architecture winning.** Four
arms have been rejected, and it may be five. That is worth saying plainly to an
autonomous session, because the temptation to keep re-architecting until
something passes is exactly how this project would produce a result it cannot
trust.

If the branch line never beats `ca906040`, the delivered outcome is still:

- a **calibrated, explainable, reproducible** vetting service, which is more
  than the reference implementation offers;
- the **label-bias work from stage 8**, which is where the largest measured
  defect actually lives and which improves any model, including the incumbent;
- an **evaluation apparatus** — variance decomposition, paired folds, the
  control arm, injection-recovery on matched hosts, per-mission gating — that
  can tell a real improvement from noise. Most of the audit's findings were
  cases where it previously could not.

The one genuine gap against ExoMiner is **distribution**: a published
survey-scale catalogue with per-row uncertainty, a DOI and a citation ask. That
is a publishing task, not an engineering one, and it is not on this roadmap.

## Non-negotiables

These survive every stage below. They are not style preferences.

1. **Environment.** `source /opt/anaconda3/etc/profile.d/conda.sh && conda
   activate exoplanet-hunter-v2` before anything. The V1 env shadows the package
   with V1 code and has silently run the wrong trainer before.
2. **Never promote.** Serving is `ca906040` on Fly, unchanged since 2026-07-19.
   Do not touch `models/registry.json`. Promotion is a human decision, always,
   however good a result looks.
3. **Guards raise.** This project's defining failure mode is a check that
   returns a plausible answer instead of failing. No broad `try/except`. A new
   guard that cannot be observed to fire is not a guard — test it by making it
   fire.
4. **One test file per source module.** No casual scripts. Scratch work goes to
   the scratchpad, not the repo.
5. **Pre-registration is binding.** Write down how a result will be read
   *before* the run finishes, in `roadmap.md`. This matters more in an
   autonomous session than a supervised one: nobody is watching to stop a
   post-hoc reading. If a result lands outside what was pre-registered, say so
   and stop — do not adjust the criterion.

The suite is slow and you will run it many times. Run the affected test file
during development and the full suite before each commit, in the background.

**Run it one process per file.** Repeated `run_cv` calls in a single process get
monotonically slower — measured 86s, 108s, 161s, 190s for four *identical* runs,
and a file with eighteen of them did not finish in three hours.
`clear_session()` does not help; the cause is open. See the audit's "still open".

```bash
for f in pipeline/tests/test_*.py; do python -m pytest "$f" -q -p no:randomly; done
```

Do not pipe a long run through `tail` — it buffers, so you get no progress for
hours. Redirect to a log and tail the log.

## Stage 6 — recall variance + re-baseline *(next, ~3–4 h)*

The immediate task, and it dissolves the queued capacity-arm repeat.

**The problem.** `recall @1% FPR` is the criterion that has rejected all four
arms — run 3 was rejected on **0.145 vs 0.307** — and it has **no variance
estimate at all**. The project measured the AUC noise floor (`seed_sd 0.0081`,
`fold_sd 0.0094`) and adopted "a margin under ~0.009 is not a decision", but
never gave the same treatment to the statistic that does the actual rejecting.

**The change.** `_variance_decomposition` (`training/train_branches.py:74`)
reads only `model_roc_auc`. Per-member metrics are computed at
`train_branches.py:290` via `classification_metrics`, whose `.recall` is recall
at threshold 0.5 — **not** the gate's statistic. The gate's
`recall_at_1pct_fpr` lives in `SliceMetrics` (`eval/comparison.py:54`), which
the audit verified exact against sklearn and a brute-force threshold sweep.

So: record each member's `recall_at_1pct_fpr` alongside its AUC, and report
`recall_seed_sd` / `recall_fold_sd` beside the existing pair. **Purely additive
to `cv_summary.json`** — the promotion gate reads named keys and is unaffected.
Add tests to `test_train_branches.py`.

**Then run the re-baseline**: HEAD, `--n-models-per-fold 3`, 5 folds, clean
tree. ~60 min. This is the control for every subsequent stage, and it returns
the recall noise floor for free.

**Pre-register before reading it.** At minimum: what recall `seed_sd` would have
to be for run 3's 0.145-vs-0.307 rejection to remain sound, and what it would
have to be for the capacity arm's 0.145 → 0.236 to have been noise. Write both
numbers down first.

**Why this replaces the queued capacity repeat.** That repeat was specified
against the capacity arm's architecture, which no longer exists on HEAD. Running
it now would measure the rebuilt branch, not the capacity arm — the same trap as
`init_filters=22` being re-derived against run 3. Checking out the old code
answers a question about a retired architecture. This measures the thing
underneath instead, on the architecture that exists, for one run instead of
three.

## Stage 7 — branch attribution *(~15–20 h)*

Absorbs old 2(b) and 2(c). The question is which of the eleven branches earn
their place, now that the unfolded one can actually see a transit.

**Build.** A branch-drop mechanism driven by config rather than by editing
`build_cnn_branches` — leaving a branch out must be a declared experiment, not a
code edit. Plus the harness the roadmap has owed since 2(b) was specified:
**injection-recovery on matched hosts with observation baseline held constant**,
which is the only causal measure available here and is immune to the
label-selection confound that moved to stage 8.

**Compute.** Eleven branches, grouped into ~7 families (flux family, unfolded,
trend, periodogram pair, centroid, gap, scalar-only), one leave-one-out CV run
each at `--n-models-per-fold 3` ≈ **7 h**. Do not run these at one model per
fold to save time; without the variance estimate the deltas are unreadable, and
that is the mistake this project keeps making.

**Its criterion is already specified and is not AUC.** Stage 2(b)'s only
surviving success criterion is the **control-arm host-pass rate: 26.4% must
fall**. It is measured on real hosts with no injection, so a pass means the
model scored the star rather than the transit. The candidate view set was
rebuilt this session specifically to unblock it. The baseline correlation was
retired as a gate and is a reported diagnostic only.

## Stage 8 — labels and negatives *(~25–35 h, lowest confidence)*

The largest measured defect in the project, and no architecture can reach it:
observation baseline correlates **+0.278 with the label itself, +0.387 on
TESS** — above every model. TESS confirmed planets have a median baseline of
1,495 d against 430 d for false positives. The model learned it because in the
training labels it is true.

Contents: EB-catalogue and brown-dwarf negatives, the ephemeris-match test,
scrambled/inverted synthetic negatives from the existing injection machinery,
plus the three interventions — propensity-score weighting on observation
baseline, baseline-stratified negative sampling, synthetic negatives that break
the correlation by construction.

**Why the estimate is soft.** It involves external catalogue ingestion, whose
only precedent here is the DV download (sized at 14–56 GB, actual 3.6 GB in
5.3 h — the estimate was 5× out in the *safe* direction, but it was out).

**Two knock-ons to budget for**, both stated in the roadmap and both easy to
forget: changing the label distribution **invalidates the re-baselined incumbent
summary** from stage 3, which needs regenerating; and it invalidates stage 7's
attribution numbers, which is exactly why stage 8 was moved ahead of the
difference-image branch.

## Stage 9 — difference-image branch *(~10–14 h)*

The only genuine *build* left in the model. Blocked on a known problem: the
stamps are **11–17 px, not the fixed 33×33** the design assumed — that is
Kepler's size — so they must be re-gridded to a fixed size first. Then a view
set rebuild (~95 min) and 2 CV runs.

## Stage 10 — Optuna re-tune *(~12–15 h, mostly unattended)*

On the winner, after the distribution is settled. Precedent from `optuna.db`:
**33 trials in 10.3 h** at `n_splits=2` on the dual-view model, ~19 min/trial.
The branch model trains at ~4 min/model, so a 2-fold trial is ~8–12 min; the
configured 50 trials lands at **~10–13 h**, plus a full 5-fold confirmation run.

Note `conf/train/tune.yaml` sets `timeout: 7200` **per invocation** and the study
resumes, so this is several invocations, not one. The existing campaign already
rejected focal loss — do not re-litigate it without a reason.

## Stage 11 — serving parity + explainability *(~10–15 h, no training)*

Per-branch contributions through `/score` via branch-occlusion; `TargetScorer`
computing every branch live. Carries the three deferred items: `score_std`
persisted and surfaced in the candidate catalogue, provenance headers in
`results/*.csv`, and precision@k. Pure engineering — no CV runs, so it can fill
time while a long run is in flight.

# Traps that will cost you a day each

Every one of these is already documented; they are collected here because they
are the ones that bite silently.

- **Two TESS populations coexist and the docs quote the smaller one.** Headline
  figures (0.9130 / 0.145 / 0.307) are the **n=2,367 gate slice**, the shared
  join used for promotion. A run's own `cv_summary.json` `per_mission.TESS`
  block covers the full TESS set and reads differently — run 3 is 0.9119 /
  0.1434 there. Both are correct. Never "fix" one to match the other; state
  which you are quoting.
- **The variance floor governs every reading.** `seed_sd 0.0081`, `fold_sd
  0.0094`, near-equal — so the old ±0.0106 was never the run's uncertainty. **An
  AUC margin under ~0.009 is not a decision.** After stage 6 you will have the
  equivalent number for recall; until then, treat recall deltas as unquantified.
- **An eager `GradientTape` over the assembled branch model aborts the
  process.** `Fatal Python error: Aborted` inside `_ConcatGradV2` on TF 2.17.1 /
  Keras 3.15.0 — no exception, no traceback, and under pytest it takes the whole
  session with it. Reproduced against HEAD's unmodified model, so it is the
  environment, not a change. `model.fit` is unaffected. Probe an isolated layer
  for per-variable gradients, as `pool_gradients` does.
- **`promotion_gate.py` reads the registry and will refuse**, because the live
  incumbent has no `per_mission` block, and it has **no `--incumbent` flag**.
  Call `evaluate_promotion` against
  `models/cv/incumbent-rebaselined/cv_summary.json` directly, or regenerate it
  with `evaluate.py summarise`.
- **A rebuilt view set trips the gate's row-count alarm** against run 3 and the
  capacity arm, which were measured on the previous catalogue. That alarm is
  **correct**. Do not silence it.
- **`summary_quality_fraction` trips the degenerate-spread warning** (MAD is
  exactly 0). Expected and deliberate. Do not suppress it.
- **Mypy's `ignore_missing_imports` is scoped, not global.** Add new unstubbed
  dependencies to the named list in `pipeline/pyproject.toml`. **Do not restore
  the global flag** — it also silenced unresolvable *first-party* imports, so a
  typo'd `exoplanet_hunter` submodule type-checked clean as `Any`.
- **The interim cache now covers candidates, not training targets.** The
  training entries were orphaned by the ephemeris key and were deleted
  2026-08-08 (395.9 MB). A training view set rebuild is cold: budget ~95 min.
- **`build_viewset.py`'s progress line reports a stale cache size** — it globs
  `cache/*.npz` while entries are written to `cache/g{G}l{L}/`. Cosmetic, but
  the number means nothing. Fix it or ignore it; do not read it.
- **A style rubric recommending `try/except: print_exception_message()` and
  `.format()` over f-strings was rejected**, with reasons in the audit. The rest
  of it — modularisation, no magic numbers, docstrings, PEP 8, no globals, no
  notebooks — is in force.

# When to stop and ask

Autonomy has edges. Stop and surface rather than deciding, for:

- **anything touching `models/registry.json` or Fly** — always, no exceptions;
- **a result that lands outside its pre-registration** — report it as
  falsified, do not re-specify the criterion;
- **abandoning or re-scoping a stage** — scaling the work down is Ollie's call;
- **deleting data** that is not re-derivable, or anything with a DVC pointer;
- **`dvc push` / `git push`** — commit freely, but pushing is Ollie's.

`dvc push` and `git push v2origin v2:main` are the outstanding manual steps from
